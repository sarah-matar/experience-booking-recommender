# Evaluation: what the numbers mean

## Reproducible experiment

The published artifact is `metrics.json`, generated by the actual default CLI run:

```sh
experience-reco generate --seed 42 --users 360 --items-per-category 100
experience-reco batch --seed 42 --epochs 45 --report-dir docs
```

The dataset has 360 users, 800 listings, 85,445 weighted events, and 2,784 search
logs. Twelve deliberately cold users have no interaction history. All catalog
metadata and home-city fields are present before the temporal cutoff.

The generator samples two hidden preferred tags per user/category plus a city and
budget preference. Observed items are sampled probabilistically from these tastes;
there are also weak events and low reviews. Latent preferences exist only in the
generator's local variables. They are not stored in the database or read by the
training pipeline. All values are invented, including vendor names.

## Temporal protocol

The last 30 days of observed events form a global test period. Events at or before
the cutoff train the evaluation model. A late review of a previously seen item is
not counted as new discovery. New positives are unique user-item pairs with positive
aggregate held-out confidence and no low review in that period. Training-observed
pairs are removed from *both* algorithms' candidate lists, irrespective of signal.

Cutoff-local profiles use only training events. Catalog metadata is available to
both approaches; there are no catalog creations after the cutoff in this fixture.
No generator tastes, future reviews, future popularity, or future user features
enter evaluation training. A single fixed configuration is used across categories:
32 factors, WARP, 45 epochs, learning rate 0.035, regularization 1e-6, seed 42,
and one training thread. Thread count is deliberately fixed for repeatability.

A second model is fit on all available data to populate serving tables. It is never
used to compute the held-out metric table. The published evaluation numbers must
not be recomputed from the already-refitted serving rankings.

## Candidate and baseline parity

For each eligible warm user, rank the complete active category catalog minus all
training-seen pairs. The popularity baseline uses the same exclusions and same
catalog. It scores capped aggregate training confidence, puts same-city items ahead
of out-of-city ones, and backfills category-wide. The hybrid can rank across cities;
city is a feature, not a hard filter in this comparison.

The city-prior baseline is stronger than unsegmented popularity. Both candidate
lists include items without interactions. This experiment does not specifically
hold out a cold-item cohort: content support is an architectural property checked
in tests, not a cold-item accuracy claim.

## Metric definitions

For user u, recommendation cutoff K=10, held-out set R, and hit indicator rel(j):

- Precision@10 = hits in the first 10 positions / 10.
- Recall@10 = hits in the first 10 positions / |R|.
- DCG@10 = sum(rel(j) / log2(j+1)) for one-based rank j.
- NDCG@10 = DCG@10 divided by the ideal DCG for min(10, |R|) positives.

Users without a held-out positive do not have a meaningful recall denominator and
are excluded; cold users are excluded from personalization evaluation. Metrics are
averaged over users within a category, then macro-averaged across categories.
`evaluated_users` is per category, not a count of distinct users across the experiment.

## Limits to discuss in an interview

1. Synthetic preferences favour the metadata representation by construction.
2. Binary relevance treats a view and a booking as equally relevant test outcomes,
   despite their different training confidence. This is not conversion prediction.
3. Cold-user and session-boost behaviour have correctness tests, not measured ranking
   uplift. No new-item holdout or online randomized comparison was conducted.
4. A negative review vetoes positives but WARP does not learn signed negative gradients.
5. There are no exposure/impression propensities; missing interactions are not proof
   of dislike. Sampled WARP negatives inherit this implicit-feedback limitation.
6. Results are point estimates without confidence intervals, significance testing,
   repeated seeds, or a separate hyperparameter validation period.
7. The catalog is tiny (100 items/category). Full scoring and dense item cosine are
   appropriate here; larger catalogs need retrieval/ANN, batching, and load testing.
8. Geographic preference is only a city label. Real distance, travel dates, capacity,
   cancellation, and inventory constraints belong in a production eligibility layer.

Recommended next evaluation: freeze a validation period, compare popularity,
content-only and hybrid on purchase relevance, then measure cold cohorts, coverage,
diversity, calibration, and exposure. Confirm real impact with an A/B experiment.
